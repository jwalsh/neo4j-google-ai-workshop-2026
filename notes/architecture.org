* What gets built

From the slide "Here's what we're building today…", redrawn as
[[file:diagrams/what-we-are-building.mmd][diagrams/what-we-are-building.mmd]]. The slide itself is not in the
repository.

Everything runs in Google Cloud.

- Cloud Storage holds the source data and is loaded into Neo4j.
- Neo4j AuraDB Professional holds the graph database. Around it: Query,
  Bloom, Graph Analytics (drawn next to Bloom, fed from the database), an
  Aura Agent, and Document Intelligence.
- Gemini Enterprise holds a Neo4j Agent, which talks to the graph database
  below it and to a Gemini Enterprise app beside it.

The three stated aims of putting a graph under the agent:

1. Reduce token usage
2. Improve accuracy
3. Increase explainability

Where the labs land on the picture: Labs 2 and 3 are the database, Lab 4 is
Query, Lab 5 is Bloom, Lab 6 is Document Intelligence, Labs 7 and 8 are the
Neo4j Agent in Gemini Enterprise, Lab 9 is the Aura Agent. No lab is named
for Graph Analytics.

* Neo4j in the Google Cloud ecosystem

From the slide of that name, redrawn as
[[file:diagrams/neo4j-in-google-cloud.mmd][diagrams/neo4j-in-google-cloud.mmd]]. Arrow directions are as the slide
draws them.

Neo4j Aura sits in the middle, holding Graph Analytics, the graph database
and Bloom. Around it, the ways in and out:

- BigQuery and Cloud Storage feed Dataflow (Native), which writes to Aura.
  Cloud Storage also has an arrow straight to Aura.
- Dataflow (JDBC) sits between BigQuery and Aura with arrows both ways.
- The Neo4j Connector for Apache Kafka, between Kafka and Aura.
- A database driver, between Cloud Functions and Aura.
- The Neo4j Connector for Apache Spark, between Dataproc and Aura.
- An arrow from Aura to Gemini.

Where today's work sits on it: Lab 4 is the straight arrow from Cloud
Storage to Aura. =src/workshop/neo4j.clj= is the database driver, without
the Cloud Functions. The Dataflow and Dataproc routes are the two things in
[[file:resources.org][Resources from the slides]]. Nothing today used Kafka.

The slide does not say what distinguishes Dataflow (Native) from Dataflow
(JDBC). The Google Cloud to Neo4j template in the resources note reads
BigQuery and Cloud Storage and writes to Neo4j, which fits the Native box;
that is a reading, not something the slide states.
