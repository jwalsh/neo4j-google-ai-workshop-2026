#+TITLE: 014 - A prompt searched for, and a model that only decides, on transit questions
#+DATE: 2026-10-09

* Source

Experiment 013 left the transit agent on a 3B model with a prompt written
by hand and three kinds of failure: a question no tool answers is answered
anyway, "To X from Y" is read backwards, and one tool is taken for
another. Two things were proposed for it on the same afternoon:

- DSPy, a Python library that takes a program of model calls, a metric and
  examples, and searches for the prompt that scores best on one model.
- Decision models, new in Ollama 0.35: =POST /v1/systemone= takes a text
  and a question with named options and returns a probability for each
  option. No text is generated. =laya= (Convai, 421M parameters, 846 MB)
  is the smallest.

* Hypothesis

Two, tested apart.

1. A search over worked examples in the prompt raises the 3B model's score
   on transit questions it has not seen, by more than run-to-run noise.
2. A decision model chooses the tool as well as the 3B chat model does, in
   a tenth of the time, and its probabilities give a threshold that says
   "none" where the chat model answers anyway.

* Method

This is the part to reuse.

1. *Questions from the graph, not from the author.* =questions.py= draws
   stations, routes and municipalities from the transit Neo4j with a fixed
   seed and puts them into fixed phrasings: ten for a journey, three per
   other tool, ten for questions no tool answers (timetables, fares,
   buses, parking, writes) and six off the subject. A station is sometimes
   written short, "Kendall" for "Kendall/MIT", when the short form still
   matches one stored name. 371 questions, split by row into 80 to train,
   80 to validate and 211 held out. Each carries the tool and parameters a
   right answer runs, so grading needs no judge.
2. *The agent as a DSPy program.* =optimize.py= has the same two steps as
   =workshop.agent=: choose a tool, then fill that tool's arguments. Tool
   names and descriptions are read from =agents/mbta.agent.json=. The
   checks around the model are copied without a database: an argument must
   appear in the question, and a name must match one stored name.
3. *One metric, the one already in use.* Right, refused or wrong, as
   experiments 009 and 013 grade. The optimizer is given "right".
4. *Baseline first.* The program with no optimizer, on the 211.
5. *Then an optimizer*, compiled on the 80 and scored on the other 80
   while it searches, and its result scored once on the 211.
6. *The decision model on the same 211*, tool choice only (=decide.py=),
   since it cannot fill a string.

All of it ran on the laptop: an M1 Pro, 16 GB, four Neo4j JVMs beside the
model. =qwen2.5:3b= through Ollama's OpenAI-compatible endpoint.

* Gate

1. The optimized program gets at least 10 more of 211 right than the
   baseline and no more wrong.
2. The decision model's tool choice is within 5 of the chat model's of 211.

* Result

Neither passed.

End to end, =qwen2.5:3b=, 211 held-out questions:

| prompt                                  | right | refused | wrong | seconds each | search |
|-----------------------------------------+-------+---------+-------+--------------+--------|
| DSPy's default, no examples             |   188 |      10 |    13 |         1.06 | none   |
| BootstrapFewShotWithRandomSearch        |   193 |       9 |     9 |         0.72 | 11.5 min |

Five more right and four fewer wrong: the right direction, half the gate,
and one run. Tool choice alone, the same 211:

| chooser                        | tool chosen | time each |
|--------------------------------+-------------+-----------|
| =qwen2.5:3b=, no examples      |         186 | about 0.5 s |
| =qwen2.5:3b=, searched examples |        187 | about 0.4 s |
| =laya=, decision model         |         106 | 83 ms     |

=laya= with a threshold on its probability:

| below this, say "none" | tool chosen of 211 | "none" said of 39 |
|------------------------+--------------------+-------------------|
|                   0.00 |                106 |                11 |
|                   0.30 |                102 |                33 |
|                   0.40 |                 84 |                39 |
|                   0.50 |                 75 |                39 |

=results-qwen2-5-3b.json= and =decisions-laya.json= have every row;
=program-qwen2-5-3b-fewshot.json= is the prompt the search kept.

* What the failures say

- The search moved failures more than it removed them. With no examples,
  nine "Where does the Orange Line go?" questions went to the tool that
  takes a station. With the searched examples two do, and five journeys
  now go to the route tool, which did not happen before. The three
  examples it kept for choosing a tool are two "what is near" and one
  "what runs through": none is a journey, a route in order, or a "none".
- Questions no tool answers are still answered. Eight wrong before, eight
  after: "How long does it take from Arlington to Wonderland?" runs a
  journey, "Which bus goes to Medford/Tufts?" runs routes at a station.
  No example of "none" was among those kept, and the metric gives the
  search no more reason to keep one than any other.
- =laya= is twelve times faster and right half the time. Its limits
  decide that: every option shares a budget of 176 tokens, so each tool
  got its name and about nine words of its description. It sends journeys
  to "Stations Near a Station" (19) and to "none" (18), and municipalities
  to "Stations Near a Station" (15 of 18).
- Its threshold does what was hoped and costs too much. At 0.4 every one
  of the 39 no-tool questions is refused, and so are most of the others.
  Its probabilities are flat: a right answer at 0.35 beside a wrong one
  at 0.34.
- The generated questions are harder than 013's eighteen in one way and
  easier in another. They are 211 and not written to be answered; but
  their phrasings are thirteen fixed strings per kind, the same in train
  and test, so a kept example can match a test question's wording
  exactly. The gain measured here is an upper bound on what a stranger's
  questions would see.

* What carries back to the Clojure agent

Nothing was ported. What the search found is a set of nine worked
examples, three per step, and the instructions unchanged. Adding examples
to =agent/instructions= is a few lines. On this evidence it is worth
five questions in two hundred and should be chosen by hand to include a
journey, a route and a "none", which the search did not do.

* Log

1. DSPy 3.4.0 against Ollama. Its own Ollama route failed (a credential
   check, then a 404); the OpenAI-compatible endpoint works. =qwen3:4b=
   could not be made to stop thinking through it, 6 to 13 seconds a call
   against 1.7 with =workshop.ollama='s =think false=, so only
   =qwen2.5:3b= was tuned.
2. =questions.py=: 371 questions, 80 / 80 / 211.
3. Baseline: 188 / 10 / 13.
4. BootstrapFewShotWithRandomSearch, 8 candidate programs, 3 bootstrapped
   and 3 labelled examples each, 691 seconds: 193 / 9 / 9.
5. MIPROv2, =auto="light"=, the 3B model proposing its own instructions.
   First run stopped on a missing dependency (optuna). The second scored
   the default program at 87.5% on validation, then took 40 minutes over
   one trial with the laptop busy, and was stopped at trial 4 of 88 with
   no better program found. Not a result.
6. =laya= pulled. The endpoint wants options as a map of key to
   description, refuses an option over 48 tokens and all options over 176.
   106 of 211.

* Not done

- MIPROv2 to completion, and with a larger model writing the candidate
  instructions. A 3B model proposing prompts for itself is the weakest
  form of it.
- =qwen3:4b=, the better of the two laptop models in 013.
- Phrasings held out, not only stations: train on some wordings, test on
  others. That is the honest test of a searched prompt.
- Options written for =laya=, short and unlike each other, in place of
  truncated descriptions written for a chat model; and =tev1:4b= or
  =nimble=, decision models with more room.
- The winning examples in =workshop.agent=, scored by experiment 013's
  runner on both models.
- Variance. One run of each.
