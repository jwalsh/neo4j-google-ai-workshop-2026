#+TITLE: 010 - A bibliography's papers as a graph, in three layers
#+DATE: 2026-10-09

* Source

The owner's survey of agentic triage, a gist cloned by ghq
(=gist.github.com/jwalsh/5ff341dadc8444f29a10b507869e59f6=): an org file and
=references.bib=, 255 entries, 27 of them arXiv papers. The gist is read
where ghq put it and nothing of it is copied here. =bb papers fetch= keeps
the PDFs and their text in =$XDG_CACHE_HOME/workshop-papers= (=bb/papers.clj=);
=src/workshop/papers.clj= reads that cache.

* Hypothesis

A reading list becomes a dataset in three layers, and the layers differ in
how far they can be trusted:

1. from the bibliography alone: Paper, Author, WROTE;
2. from the papers' text with no model: CITES, where one paper's text holds
   another's title or arXiv identifier;
3. from the text with a small model: MENTIONS, the benchmarks and datasets a
   paper names, each kept only if the text contains it.

* Gate

Layers one and two load into Neo4j, a second load changes nothing, and the
most-cited paper within the set is one a reader of the field would expect.
Layer three: at least three quarters of what it keeps are benchmarks or
datasets, judged by reading the list.

* Result

Layers one and two passed. Layer three did not.

| layer | what                         | count | how it was checked                                   |
|-------+------------------------------+-------+------------------------------------------------------|
|     1 | papers, authors, WROTE       | 27, 123, 134 | counts equal the bibliography's; second load identical |
|     2 | CITES within the set         |    17 | read 12 of the 17; SWE-bench cited by 5, Agentless by 4 |
|     3 | MENTIONS, one model, one pass |    59 | read all 59; 23 are benchmarks or datasets           |

=graph.edn= is layers one and two. =mentions.edn= is layer three as the
model gave it, model =qwen2.5-coder:7b-instruct-q4_K_M= on another host,
27 calls, median 6.6 s.

* What layer three got wrong

Nothing was invented: no name was dropped by the text check, in 27 papers.
What it kept that is not a benchmark:

- people and addresses from the author block ("Celal Ziftci",
  "mnbucher@stanford.edu", "Stanford University");
- the paper's own system ("COTA", "TickIt", "LocAgent", "Cupid");
- models ("BERT", "ChatGPT", "Claude-3-5-sonnet-20241022");
- section-like phrases ("Working Memory", "Face Recognition").

So the check that worked for the agent's arguments (009) proves less here.
There, "in the question" was the whole requirement. Here, "in the text" is
necessary and nowhere near enough: the model was asked for a kind of thing,
and the text contains every kind.

And what it got right does not join: "SWE-bench", "SWE-Bench",
"SWE-bench Lite", "SWE-Bench-Lite" and "SWE-bench Lite LeaderBoard" are five
nodes. Only two names are shared by two papers. The citation layer, which
needed no model, connects the same papers better.

* Log

1. =bb papers fetch=: 27 of 27, 48 MB, three seconds apart. Run again: all
   cached, no request made.
2. The bibliography parser reads one field per line. It took "and others"
   as an author named "others"; filtered in =papers/graph=.
3. CITES by the first 45 letters of a title, compared after dropping
   everything but letters and digits, or by the arXiv identifier. 17 edges.
   Not checked for misses: a paper cited under a different title, or a
   reference list the PDF's text extraction mangled, would not be found.
4. MENTIONS over the first 6,000 characters of each paper. One question,
   one model, one pass.
5. Loaded into the embedded Neo4j beside the Form 13 graph; the second load
   returned the same five counts.

* Not done

- Layer three with a typed answer, e.g. each name with a kind from an enum,
  so that "benchmark" is the model's claim and can be filtered.
- Canonical names: fold case and punctuation, and treat "X Lite" as a
  variant of "X" or not, on purpose.
- The other 228 entries. They are web pages; fetching them is a different
  job with different terms per site.
- References parsed out of each PDF, which would give citations to papers
  outside the set.
- A Gemini agent. The idea came from the workshop's Gemini Enterprise labs;
  that account had expired, so the model here was a local one.
