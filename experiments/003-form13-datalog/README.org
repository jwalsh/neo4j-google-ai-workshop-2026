#+TITLE: 003 - Form 13 as datoms, queried with Datalog
#+DATE: 2026-10-08

* Source

Lab 4's Form 13 day file, =data/form13-2023-05-11.csv= (7,381 rows, fetched
as =src/workshop/form13.clj= does; git-ignored). The lab loads it into Neo4j
as =(:Manager)-[:OWNS {value, shares, reportCalendarOrQuarter}]->(:Company)=;
the statements are in =labs/lab-04/=. The known numbers are from
=workshop.form13= over the same file.

The owner's question: what are related ways to hold data like this, such as
RDF-style tuples, and how is that explored from Emacs with CIDER. RDF proper
is =experiments/002-form13-rdf/=. This is the Clojure-native end: stores of
[entity attribute value] tuples queried with Datalog.

* Hypothesis

The property graph is not needed to ask the lab's questions. The same file as
flat three-part tuples in an in-memory Clojure store gives the same counts and
the same degree tables, and a Datalog rule says "managers who hold the same
companies" at least as plainly as the Cypher pattern does.

* What was kept and what was left out

Kept: every row and every column. Two stores, the same questions of each:
DataScript 1.8.1 as the main one, Asami 2.3.4 as the comparison.

Changed, deliberately:

- A datom has three parts and no room for properties on an edge. =OWNS=
  becomes an entity of its own, a holding, with a ref to the manager and a
  ref to the company, and value, shares and quarter as its attributes.
- =:manager/name= and =:company/cusip= are unique identities, so loading is
  upsert: 7,381 manager maps and 7,381 company maps, none with an id,
  collapse to 28 and 2,251 entities.
- =:company/name= is cardinality-many. With CUSIP as the identity and the
  default cardinality, each upsert replaces the name and the 258 CUSIPs with
  more than one spelling come out as 0: no error, the last name filed wins.
  That is measured in the gate (=lossy-schema=), not assumed. As a set, every
  spelling stays on the company. The holding also keeps the spelling its own
  filing used (=:holding/company-name=), so which manager wrote "Apple, Inc."
  is still answerable.
- The quarter stays a string. All 7,381 rows say =2023-03-31=.

Left out: Neo4j, any network, persistence (both stores are in memory and gone
when the JVM exits), Datalevin and XTDB (Asami resolved and ran, so the
fallbacks were not needed and were not tried), transaction history and
=as-of= (DataScript keeps none), and the value scale question from
=workshop.form13/odd-prices=.

* Gate

=clojure -M:gate= exits 0 only if all 23 checks pass. Against the known
numbers, in both stores: 28 managers, 2,251 CUSIPs, 7,381 holdings, 258
CUSIPs with more than one name, Envestnet at the top with 1,615 companies;
in DataScript also Apple (CUSIP 037833100) held by 22 managers. Against the
same answers computed from the parsed rows with =group-by=, no database: the
top five of each degree table, row for row, and the shared-company count of
every one of the 281 manager pairs that share anything.

* Property graph, datoms, triples: this file in each

experiment and no Neo4j timing is claimed.

What that came to in practice:

- The relationship with properties is the one thing the graph has that the
  tuples lack, and it costs one hop. Cypher writes
  =(a:Manager)-[:OWNS]->(c:Company)<-[:OWNS]-(b:Manager)=; here the same
  walk passes through two holding entities. The =holds= rule buys the edge
  back, and after that =co-holds= reads much like the Cypher. If =OWNS= had
  no properties, =:manager/holds= as a cardinality-many ref would be the
  graph's edge exactly and the holding entity would not exist.
- Identity is where the three differ most, and the name variants show it.
  Neo4j's =ON CREATE SET= keeps the first name and drops the rest quietly.
  DataScript with the obvious schema keeps the last and drops the rest
  quietly: 0 variants instead of 258. One word in the schema keeps them all.
  Asami keeps them all without being asked, because it has no notion of a
  single-valued attribute; whether that is a feature depends on wanting it.
- Cypher is the better reporting language. Ordering, limits, and filtering
  on an aggregate are in the query; in Datalog the query ends at a set of
  tuples and the rest is =sort-by= and =take=. In a REPL that is no loss:
  the result is Clojure data and the next form is Clojure. Set semantics is
  the trap that goes with it: a sum needs =:with=, and Asami's sum was wrong
  by 3.5 billion without saying so.
- The database is a value. =db-with= on the loaded database gives a new one
  with a fourth Apple name and leaves the first alone, which is what makes
  the =lossy-schema= comparison three lines. Nothing in the lab's Aura
  instance works like that.
- Between the two stores: DataScript did what its documentation says on
  every query and took about nine times longer to load. Asami loaded fast and
  is closer to RDF in spirit (no schema, any value as a node, open-ended
  attributes), but five of the first queries written against it were
  silently wrong, threw, or did not return. For this data, explored from a
  REPL, DataScript is the one to reach for.

* What it is not

Not a comparison of performance with Neo4j: nothing was timed there. Not a
test of either store at a size where it matters; 7,381 rows fit in a
=group-by=, which is what the gate checks them against. Not a durable
database: Datalevin and XTDB were not tried, and DataScript holds no history,
so the "database as a value in time" half of the Datomic idea is absent.
Not an account of why Asami's aggregates behave as they do: the behaviour was
observed on 2.3.4 and worked around, the source was not read. Not RDF: no
IRIs, no vocabulary, no SPARQL; that is experiment 002. And the CIDER
workflow was checked by loading and evaluating the forms from a script and by
batch Emacs, not by anyone pressing =C-x C-e=.
