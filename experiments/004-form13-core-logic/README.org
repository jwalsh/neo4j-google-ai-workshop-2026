#+TITLE: 004 - Form 13 in core.logic
#+DATE: 2026-10-08

* Source

The Form 13 day file from Lab 4, =data/form13-2023-05-11.csv= (git-ignored;
=workshop.form13/fetch!= at the root downloads it). 7,381 rows, one quarter. The lab
loads it into Neo4j as =(:Manager)-[:OWNS]->(:Company)=;
=src/workshop/form13.clj= at the root answers the lab's questions from the
file in memory, and its numbers are the oracle here.

Libraries, from Maven Central's =maven-metadata.xml= on 2026-10-08:
=org.clojure/core.logic 1.1.1= and =org.clojure/data.csv 1.1.1=, the newest
of each. Clojure 1.12.0, as at the root.

* Hypothesis

The same file, loaded as facts into core.logic's fact database (pldb),
answers the lab's questions with relations and gives the same numbers. The
relational part will be short; counting will not be relational at all; and
joins over 7,381 facts will be slow enough somewhere to be worth writing
down.

All three held. The slow part was not where expected.

* What was kept and what was left out

Kept: three relations.

| relation                                   | facts | indexed             |
|--------------------------------------------+-------+---------------------|
| =manager(manager-name)=                    |    28 | manager-name        |
| =company(cusip, company-name)=             | 2,577 | cusip, company-name |
| =holds(cusip, manager-name, value, shares)= | 7,381 | cusip, manager-name |

=company= is one fact per name a CUSIP was filed under, which is why it has
2,577 facts for 2,251 CUSIPs. =value= is a long; it is integral throughout
the file.

On indexing. pldb keeps one map per indexed position and, for each call,
uses the index of the leftmost position that is both indexed and ground. One
index per call, chosen by position, not by selectivity. So =holds= has cusip
first: when a join reaches its second =holds= with cusip and manager both
ground, the cusip bucket has at most 28 facts and the manager bucket up to
1,615. The brief suggested =holds(manager, cusip, value, shares)=; that
order was measured and is a hundred times slower on the join (Log).

Left out: the quarter (one value in the file), the filing id, and matching a
company by a fragment of its name. =form13/holders-of= at the root takes
"nvidia"; here the name is exact. A substring test is not a relation; it can
be bolted on with =project= and a predicate, and then it only runs one way.

* Where the relational part ends

core.logic has no aggregation: no count, no sum, no order, no limit other
than =run n=, no distinct. =run*= returns a vector of answers, one per
derivation, and Clojure takes it from there. In =src/form13/logic.clj= every
query function is a =run*= form bound to =answers= and then a =->>= pipeline,
so the line is on the page:

| question                    | relational (=run*=)                | Clojure, after          |
|-----------------------------+------------------------------------+-------------------------|
| how many managers           | =manager=                          | =count=                 |
| how many CUSIPs             | =company=, 2,577 answers           | =distinct=, =count=     |
| who holds APPLE INC         | =company= joined to =holds=        | =sort-by= value         |
| what two managers both hold | =holdso= twice, joined to =company= | =group-by= cusip        |
| CUSIPs with several names   | =company= twice and =!==           | =group-by= cusip        |
| manager with most companies | =holdso=, all 7,381 pairs          | =frequencies=, =sort-by= |
| largest positions           | =holds=, all 7,381 facts           | =sort-by=, =take=       |

For the last two the relational part is "give me everything". That is the
honest shape of an aggregate here.

* Gate

=clojure -M:gate= prints 17 checks and exits 1 if any is off. Eight are the
known numbers: 28 managers, 2,251 CUSIPs, 7,381 holdings, 258 CUSIPs under
more than one name, ENVESTNET ASSET MANAGEMENT INC on top with 1,615 (asked
two ways), APPLE INC held by 22 (asked two ways). The other nine compare a
relational answer with the same thing worked out from the rows with sets or
=filter=, so the constraint and chain examples are checked against something
that is not core.logic.

* Run

From this directory; the CSV path is relative to it.

#+begin_src sh
cd experiments/004-form13-core-logic
clojure -M:gate                       # or: clojure -M:gate path/to/form13.csv
clojure -M:dev:repl                   # nREPL for cider-connect

From Emacs: open =dev/explore_logic.clj=, =cider-jack-in= (the
=.dir-locals.el= here selects =clojure-cli= and the =:dev= alias), load the
buffer, and evaluate the forms in each =(comment ...)= one at a time with
=C-x C-e=.

* What core.logic gives here

Run on this data; all of it is in =dev/explore_logic.clj=.

- One relation, every direction. =holdso= is two lines. With the manager
  ground it lists holdings (Envestnet: 1,615); with the CUSIP ground it lists
  holders (Apple: 22); with neither it lists all 7,381 pairs; with both it
  is a yes or no. Nothing was written per direction. Cypher's =MATCH= does
  this too for a single pattern; the difference is that here the pattern is
  a named function that composes, so =holds-companyo=, =both-holdo= and
  =chaino= are built from it and inherit the property.
- The path is an ordinary answer. =chaino= relates a start, an end, a hop
  limit and the list of managers between. Leave the path fresh and get
  paths; supply a path with a hole in it and get what fits the hole.
- A bounded chain was worth having, barely. 281 of the 378 manager pairs
  share a holding directly. Westwood Global holds two companies and shares
  them with two managers (Envestnet and Nomura); the other 25 are two links
  away. Everything is within two links of everything, so there is no third
  hop to find. One day of filings from 28 managers, one of which holds 72%
  of all the CUSIPs, is a small-world graph with nothing in it to search.
- Constraints. =!== on the two names of a CUSIP is the real one. =nafc=
  gives "Guggenheim holds it and Envestnet does not" (219, equal to the set
  difference). A finite domain on =shares= works (191 Envestnet positions of
  between one and a hundred million shares) and is a poor fit: nothing is
  solved for, it is =filter= with more machinery. Finite domains earn their
  keep when unknowns are related by arithmetic, and a table of reported
  holdings has no unknowns.

* Beside a graph database

Fits: a few thousand facts already in a Clojure process, questions that are
the same pattern asked from different ends, rules built from other rules,
and wanting the answer as plain data in the REPL without a server. The whole
experiment is one file and two jars.

Does not fit:

- Anything aggregated. Degree centrality, which is the point of Lab 5, is
  =frequencies= here; the logic engine contributes nothing to it.
- Anything large. pldb is a map of sets in memory with one index used per
  call and no planner. Goal order and argument order are the query plan and
  the author writes both. Neo4j with a constraint on =cusip= does not ask
  that.
- Reachability at scale. No tabling was used; pldb answers are bags, so a
  pair linked through 1,099 companies is 1,099 answers unless the relation
  is cut with =onceo=, which gives up running it in every direction.
- Persistence, concurrency, Bloom, a second user.

So: a way to think about the data and to prototype a rule, next to the
database, not a replacement for it.

- 2026-10-08. Index order. The Envestnet-and-Guggenheim join with =holds=
  stored manager first: 1,099 answers in 758.8 ms. Cusip first: 8.2 ms. Same
  facts, same answers; three other runs gave 776 to 865 ms against 7 to 15.
  The cause is the leftmost-ground-index rule, read from
  =clojure/core/logic/pldb.clj= in the 1.1.1 jar.
- 2026-10-08. =!== placement. =name-variants= states =!== before the names
  are bound and takes about 100 ms. With =!== after the two =company= calls
  the same 258 came back in about 30 ms, measured in a scratch script that
  is not kept. Left as it is, since it is the one place the constraint is
  shown doing what a filter cannot; the docstring says so.
- 2026-10-08. The impractical one. All pairs within two links, with =onceo=
  at each link: 784 pairs (28 x 28; a manager reaches itself in two), 9 s on
  one run and 18 to 24 s on three others. A set intersection per pair does
  the same in milliseconds. Without =onceo= the two-link join has
  114,962,418 derivations for those pairs, a figure worked out from set
  sizes, not by running it. Bounded: =run 10000= took 0.24 s and reached 53
  distinct pairs, =run 100000= 1.3 s and 137, =run 1000000= 16.5 s and 417.
  At that rate the full enumeration is on the order of half an hour, to
  learn that everyone is connected. It was not run to the end. Both forms
  are in the last-but-one comment block of =dev/explore_logic.clj=; neither
  is in the gate.
- 2026-10-08. Bags. Westwood holds two CUSIPs and =holds-companyo= returns
  three company names, because Credicorp was filed as "CREDICORP LTD" and
  "Credicorp Ltd". Every count in this experiment that goes through
  =company= needs =distinct= for that reason.
- 2026-10-08. =dev/explore_logic.clj= loads with =(require 'explore-logic)=
  under =-M:dev=, and a script read the file and evaluated all 46 forms
  inside its comment blocks in order without error. =.dir-locals.el= was
  read by =emacs --batch= with CIDER loaded and all four variables reported
  safe.

* What it is not

Not exercised from CIDER. No interactive Emacs here: =cider-jack-in=,
=C-x C-e= and what =print-table= looks like in the REPL buffer are untested.
What was tested is the paragraph above, which is weaker. In particular,
whether CIDER takes this directory or the repository root as the project
when both have a =deps.edn= was not checked; if it picks the root, the
relative CSV path is wrong and =(form13/read-rows "data/...")= with an
explicit path is the way round.

Not compared with Neo4j, SPARQL or DataScript on the same machine. The
remarks on Cypher above are from the lab, not from a measurement, and
experiments 002 and 003 were not read.

Not a benchmark. One machine, one run for the table, =System/nanoTime=
around each call, five repetitions, no criterium.

Not idiomatic at the edges. =onceo= and =nafc= are not pure relations:
=co-holdero= grounds both managers first so that committing to one answer is
safe, and it would be wrong if called with that guarantee removed. core.logic
has tabling, which is the usual answer to duplicate derivations in a
recursive relation; it was not tried.
